Your gross pay for the year, before Income Tax and National Insurance. Include any bonus. Your P60 or your final payslip of the tax year shows it as "pay" or "taxable pay to date".

Where to find it
Where to find itOn your payslip, your P60, or in the HMRC app. Most people are on 1257L, which means you can earn £12,570 before paying Income Tax. A different number means HMRC has adjusted your tax-free amount, for example for a company car or an underpayment from last year. Codes starting with S are Scottish; a K code means you owe extra.

Assumptions
  • Your tax includes Income Tax and National Insurance on salary, and dividend tax on dividends. If you enter your own tax code, it is applied to your salary; any tax-free allowance your salary does not use counts towards your dividends.
  • Two different years. Your tax is worked out at this year's (2026/27) rates. The spending split is from 2024/25, the most recent year the government has published. A year's split can only be worked out after that year ends, so it is always a year or two behind. The shares change only slowly, and we update the split as soon as a newer one is published.
  • How your tax is split. We give each area the same share of your tax as its share of government spending. For example, the government spent £241.8 billion on health out of £1,156.2 billion in total: £241.8bn ÷ £1,156.2bn = 0.2091, so 20.9p of every £1 of your tax is counted as health. HM Treasury also shows rounded percentages, but they add up to 100.1%, so using them would split slightly more than all of your tax; using the actual £ billion amounts splits it exactly.
  • The split is illustrative: taxes are not earmarked for particular services, and spending is also paid for by other taxes and by borrowing (a £153 billion deficit in 2024/25).
  • Covers Income Tax and National Insurance only, like HMRC's summary. VAT, Council Tax, student loan repayments and your employer's National Insurance are not included.
  • Figures are worked to the penny. Each area is rounded to the nearest penny; if the rounded areas differ from your total by a penny or two, that difference is shown as its own line so everything adds up exactly.

2. How it is split

In 2024/25, the latest year published, the government spent £246.1bn + £241.8bn + £137.8bn + £124.7bn + £118.7bn + £63.6bn + £51.4bn + £46.4bn + £40.4bn + £23.0bn + £22.3bn + £17.1bn + £14.5bn + £7.7bn + £0.7bn = £1,156.2bn across the 15 areas. Each area gets the same share of your tax as its share of that spending: your tax × the area's spending ÷ total spending. For health: £241.8bn ÷ £1,156.2bn = 0.2091, or 20.9p in every £1, and your share is £6,280.40 × £241.8bn ÷ £1,156.2bn = £1,313.44. Each line in the list shows the same working for its area.
