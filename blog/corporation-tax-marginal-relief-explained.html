Corporation Tax marginal relief explained: why 26.5% is not what you pay

By Spencer Elbert · 3 November 2026 · 5 minute read ·

Corporation Tax is 19% on small profits and 25% on large ones, yet each extra pound in between costs 26.5p. Here is why, what you actually pay, and how to use it.

The short version

  • Corporation Tax is 19% on profits up to £50,000 and 25% from £250,000.
  • In between, marginal relief gradually removes the small-company discount.
  • That makes each extra pound in the middle band cost 26.5p: the 25% rate plus 1.5% of discount withdrawn.
  • But the overall rate never exceeds 25%: on £100,000 of profit it is £22,750 ÷ £100,000 = 22.75%.
  • Reducing profit in the middle band saves the most: a £10,000 pension contribution saves £2,650 there.
  • Associated companies share the limits, so marginal relief starts sooner.

Corporation Tax is 19% on small profits and 25% on large ones. In between, the rules say each extra pound of profit is effectively taxed at 26.5%, more than either. That sounds wrong, and it is one of the most common questions directors ask us. The good news: nobody actually pays 26.5% on their profit. Here is what is really going on, with the sums.

The three bands

  • Profits up to £50,000: the small profits rate of 19%.
  • Profits of £250,000 or more: the main rate of 25%.
  • Profits in between: the 25% main rate, reduced by marginal relief. HMRC's formula for the relief is 3/200 × (£250,000 − your profits).

Why a 26.5% rate appears

Think of the 19% rate as a discount for small companies. At £50,000 of profit, the discount is worth the whole difference between the two rates: £50,000 × (25% − 19%) = . Marginal relief then takes that discount away gradually as profits grow, until it has gone completely at £250,000.

The £3,000 discount is withdrawn evenly across the £200,000 between the limits:

  • Discount withdrawn for each extra pound of profit: £3,000 ÷ £200,000 = .
  • Tax on each extra pound in the middle band: the 25% main rate plus the 1.5% being withdrawn, 25% + 1.5% = .

A quicker way to work it out

For a single company with a 12-month period, there is an easier method that gives the same answer: 19% on the first £50,000, plus 26.5% on everything above it. For £100,000 of profit:

  • The first £50,000: £50,000 × 19% = .
  • The rest: (£100,000 − £50,000) × 26.5% = £50,000 × 26.5% = .

Why it matters: the 26.5% band is where planning pays most

Because each extra pound of profit in the middle band costs 26.5p in tax, anything that reduces profit in that band saves 26.5p in every pound, more than at any other level of profit. A £10,000 company pension contribution, for example, saves:

  • £10,000 × 19% = for a company with profits under £50,000,
  • £10,000 × 26.5% = for a company with profits between £50,000 and £250,000,
  • £10,000 × 25% = for a company with profits over £250,000.

The same applies to equipment purchases, salaries and other allowable costs. Timing them into a year where profits sit in the middle band gets the most relief. Our pension guide explains how company pension contributions work.

More than one company, or a short year

The £50,000 and £250,000 limits are shared between associated companies: companies under common control, such as two companies owned by the same director. Common questions

Is Corporation Tax 26.5%?

Not on your whole profit. 26.5% is the rate on each extra pound of profit between £50,000 and £250,000. The overall rate on all your profit rises gradually from 19% to 25%: on £100,000 of profit it is £22,750 ÷ £100,000 = .

What is marginal relief?

A reduction in Corporation Tax for companies with profits between £50,000 and £250,000. Tax is worked out at 25%, then reduced by 3/200 × (£250,000 − profits), so the overall rate rises smoothly from 19% to 25%.

How much Corporation Tax is due on £100,000 of profit?

Why does marginal relief make planning more valuable?

Because each extra pound of profit between £50,000 and £250,000 costs 26.5p in tax, reducing profit in that band saves 26.5p in every pound. A £10,000 pension contribution saves £10,000 × 26.5% = there, compared with £1,900 at 19%.

Do associated companies affect marginal relief?

Yes. The £50,000 and £250,000 limits are shared between associated companies. With one associated company, each company's limits are halved to £25,000 and £125,000, so higher rates start sooner.
