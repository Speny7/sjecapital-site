Ask five accountants what they charge and you will get five different answers, often with no clear explanation of what is included. This guide sets out what drives the price, what a growing limited company actually needs, and exactly what our plans cost and include, with the sums shown.

What drives the price

  • How many transactions you have. More sales, purchases and bank lines mean more bookkeeping.
  • VAT. Once your taxable turnover passes £90,000 you must register, and quarterly VAT returns add regular work.
  • Payroll. Each employee adds monthly payroll, pension contributions and HMRC submissions.
  • Reporting. Monthly management accounts take more work than a once-a-year set of accounts.
  • Complexity. Stock, several companies, overseas trade or research and development claims all add time.
  • The state of your records. Tidy, up-to-date books cost less to look after than a year's worth of receipts scattered across your inbox, your camera roll and three banking apps.

What a growing company needs

A company turning over £100,000 or more usually needs more than a set of year-end accounts. It is likely to be VAT-registered, to employ people, and to need up-to-date figures to make decisions. A complete service typically covers monthly bookkeeping, VAT returns, payroll and pensions, year-end accounts and the Corporation Tax return, the director's Self Assessment return, and planning of salary, dividends and pension contributions.

The real cost after tax

Accountancy fees are a business expense, so they reduce the company's Corporation Tax. On the Scale plan with a 12-month agreement:

  • At the 19% small profits rate: £9,180 × 19% = £1,744.20 of Corporation Tax saved, so the cost after tax is £9,180 − £1,744.20 = £7,435.80 a year, or £7,435.80 ÷ 12 = £619.65 a month.
  • With profits between £50,000 and £250,000, where marginal relief means each extra £1 of profit is taxed at 26.5%: £9,180 × 26.5% = £2,432.70 saved, so the cost after tax is £9,180 − £2,432.70 = £6,747.30 a year, or £6,747.30 ÷ 12 = £562.275, which is £562.28 a month to the nearest penny.

What a good accountant should save you

The fee is only half of the picture. Good advice should pay for itself, for example:

  • Pension planning. £10,000 of profit paid into your pension by the company puts the full £10,000 to work, while the same profit taken as a higher rate dividend leaves £5,204.25: £10,000 − £5,204.25 = £4,795.75 more working for you. Our pension guide shows the full working.
  • Avoiding penalties. For Corporation Tax returns due from 1 April 2026, filing late costs £200 straight away and another £200 at three months: £200 + £200 = £400, before any penalties based on the tax owed.
  • Your time. Every hour you spend on bookkeeping and HMRC is an hour not spent on customers.

How to compare quotes

  • Is the fee fixed, or can extra work be billed by the hour?
  • Exactly which returns are included: VAT, payroll, Corporation Tax, confirmation statement, your own Self Assessment?
  • How often will you get management accounts, and will someone explain them?
  • Who will you actually deal with, and how quickly do they reply?
  • What happens when your company grows: does the price change automatically, or by agreement?

Common questions
